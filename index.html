Emergent Urban Social Resilience

How do social actors, relations, and practices gain agency in resilience-building through inclusive, collaborative, and participatory planning? Grounded in socio-material planning and care ethics, this PhD investigates how regenerative green-social infrastructures (GSIs) are co-created and sustained as resilient urban commons.

Guiding Perspective

The Care Ethics Framework

A fundamental perspective named in the project call is the ethic of care: examining who cares for what, who is cared for, and who is left out.

  • Caring about: Recognising unattended needs in open space .
  • Taking care of: Assuming collective responsibility rather than deferring .
  • Care-giving: The direct material labor of watering, pruning, clearing .
  • Care-receiving: Experiencing psychological safety and restorative greenery .
  • Caring with: Democratic solidarity and mutual trust in urban commons .

Operationalising Agency Author's Working Proposal [✎]

Four-Pillar Analytical Agency Matrix

To rigorously trace agency over time without reducing it to attendance counts, this proposal combines three foundational models:

1. Decision Rungs :

Consultation $\rightarrow$ Advisory influence $\rightarrow$ Shared partnership $\rightarrow$ Delegated citizen control.

2. Democracy Cube :

Who participates (selection), how they communicate (deliberation mode), and extent of municipal authority.

3. Temporal Chordal Triad :

Iterational (routines), projective (vision), and practical-evaluative (responding to emergent stress).

4. Spatial Control :

The recognized legal and practical right to alter, plant, maintain, and adapt the material grounds.

Emergent Urban Social Resilience: Mapping Agency in Actor Landscapes and Co-Designing Regenerative Green-Social Infrastructures as Urban Commons

Aalborg University · Department of Sustainability and Planning · Horizon Europe DUT WeGrowCircle Consortium

1. Aim and Research Questions

Aim: To understand and strengthen how urban social resilience emerges from the interplay of social, institutional, and material-spatial dynamics, by mapping how actors gain agency and co-designing regenerative green-social infrastructures (GSIs) as urban commons in close dialogue with local planning practice.

Main Research Question: How do social actors, relations, and practices gain agency in producing urban social resilience through the interplay of social, institutional, and material-spatial dimensions, and how can mapping, co-design, and planning processes strengthen this in regenerative GSIs?
  • SRQ1 (Mapping): What actor landscapes, relations, practices, and spatial conditions shape resilience around GSIs in the Aalborg Living Lab, and where are the gaps in agency and inclusion?
  • SRQ2 (Co-Design): How can an inclusive co-design process build agency and the capacities for resilience among diverse actors?
  • SRQ3 (Stewardship & Planning): Which stewardship and governance arrangements sustain resilience, and how can they be embedded in local planning and climate action?

2. Theoretical Grounding & Guiding Care Perspective

Resilience is framed not as an engineered return to equilibrium, but as an emergent capacity of social-ecological systems . Relational planning theory recognizes that urban dynamics are produced through overlapping webs of social relations, statutory institutions, and material configurations. Elinor Ostrom's eight design principles provide an analytical lens for collective land stewardship. Crucially, the project adopts a care ethics lens to investigate who cares for what, who is cared for, and who is excluded from green infrastructure.

3. Research Design & 4-Phase Longitudinal Methodology

  • Phase 1 (M1–9): Relational Diagnosis & Baseline Mapping. Participatory GIS, document reviews, semi-structured interviews, and baseline SNA in Aalborg.
  • Phase 2 (M10–20): Inclusive Co-Creation & Boundary Prototyping. Generative mapping charrettes , physical on-site mockups, and stewardship charters.
  • Phase 3 (M21–30): Longitudinal Monitoring, Co-Evaluation & Re-Mapping. Repeated network mapping to trace changes in agency, citizen science biodiversity audits, and multi-dimensional WeGrowCircle indicator tracking.
  • Phase 4 (M31–36): Synthesis & Statutory Planning Pathways. Policy translation workshops with Aalborg Municipality, formulating amendments for Local Plans (Lokalplaner) and maintenance service agreements.

4. Context: Aalborg Living Lab

Note: Exact site to be confirmed with the WeGrowCircle team; Aalborg Øst is shown as a schematic illustrative setting (not to scale). Aalborg Øst underwent significant physical and social regeneration between 2011 and 2021 under a housing-association-led model with active tenant democracy . Rather than assuming baseline deprivation, this research asks: what agency do diverse actors have over green commons in an already-regenerated district, and how can co-management prevent post-grant abandonment?

Methodological Foundation

Why Social Network Analysis (SNA)?

Conventional stakeholder analysis assumes static categories (e.g. "residents", "planners"). Social Network Analysis (SNA) investigates actual structural relations: who shares resources, who holds authority, and where friction blocks collaboration.

Following Latour and Puig de la Bellacasa , this project extends SNA to include non-human actors (soil, stormwater retention swale, tree canopy, pollinators) to evaluate how human care practices recursively shape ecological health.

Toy Model Logic

How the Pathway Model Works

This is an interactive conceptual simulation (not empirical field data) designed to illustrate the projectification critique in under 10 seconds:

  • Grant-Led Curve: Initial funding creates high care capacity. After Year 2 grant expiry, capacity collapses exponentially due to municipal budget cuts and lack of citizen ownership.
  • Commons Curve: Starts with relationship building. Its slope and sustained capacity are dynamically modified by the 3 governance switches: Decision Rights (+15 to +25), Stewardship Charter (+15), and Planning Anchoring (+15).
